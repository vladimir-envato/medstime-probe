## Goal

MM-030 removed old medication categories and split the forms of each category into general forms
and more specific forms. Explore the medication-type screen and every category's form screen, add
medications that cover the cases below, and check that categories, forms, dose units, icons, and
the Schedule group summaries are correct and consistent everywhere. Work out what you see
yourself; the rules below say what is correct, not what every screen reads.

### About the app

- Add Medication is a series of steps: name (with catalog suggestions), medication type, dose,
  times, and a summary. The medication-type screen lists categories in sections; tapping a
  category opens its form screen, unless the category has no forms.
- A form screen has a first section with the general form(s) and a second section with more
  specific forms. The header of the first section is singular with one row and plural with more.
- A medication's category, form, and dose unit appear again on the dose step, the summary, the
  Medications tab, and the medication details.
- The Schedule tab shows medications by time. Medications at the same time form a group whose
  summary line names their forms.

### Rules of correct behavior

- Every category has an icon and a translated title. The old categories (Syrup, Eye dropper,
  Nasal spray, Ointment, Pestle, Vitamine, Mouthwash, Suppository, Solution, Suspension, Other,
  Other type, Pack) no longer exist as categories; some survive as forms inside a category, which
  is correct.
- A category that is itself one form (Tablet, Drops, Injection, and the like) has one general row:
  the category. A category named by a route (Oral, Rectal, Vaginal) has one general row, the
  route's medicine. A category that lists several forms (Cream / Gel / Ointment, Powder /
  Granules, ...) or an area (Skin / Hygiene) lists those forms as its general forms.
- Every label appears once per form screen, across both sections; no specific form repeats a
  general form or the category name. Inhaler once listed Metered Dose Inhaler twice, and Injection
  once listed a specific form named just "Injection": check those two especially.
- Each specific form fits its category (Nasal Spray under Spray, Eye Drops under Drops, Mouthwash
  under Oral, Toothpaste under Skin / Hygiene, ...).
- After a form is chosen, the category row on the type screen shows the chosen specific form
  under its title; with a general form it shows no second line repeating the category.
- The group summary names each medication by its general form, never its more specific form
  (Topical Lotion counts as lotion, Mouthwash as oral medicine, Toothpaste as hygiene product),
  in the singular for one medication of that form and the plural for more.
- The dose unit fits the form and agrees with the amount (1 vs. more than 1, and 1.5).
- In English the word "preparation" appears nowhere; Serbian uses "preparat" for the route
  categories, which is correct.

### Intended, not findings

- Ampoule, Infusion, and Medicine have no forms: tapping them selects the row and Next goes on.
- Medicine sits alone in the last section of the type screen.
- Rows in an expanded group show name and status only, with no dose.
- Details may show the more specific form (for example Topical Lotion) where the summary shows
  the general one.

If steps run low, skip form screens you already read rather than a medication.

### Always watch

On every screen, read all text and report anything inconsistent, even if no rule above mentions
it: a raw key (`medication-type-...`, `medication-specific-type-...`, `medication-dose-form-...`,
`medication-time-row...`), a format code, a blank label, a missing or placeholder icon, mixed
languages, a wrong singular or plural, a count that does not match the screen, or the same thing
named differently in two places.
