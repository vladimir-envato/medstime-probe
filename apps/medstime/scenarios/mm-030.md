## Goal

MM-030 removed old, unused medication categories and their texts and icons. Check
that every category, subcategory, icon, and text that remains is correct, and that
nothing shows a raw key such as `medication-type-...`, `medication-dose-form-...`,
or `medication-time-row...`, a blank label, a placeholder icon, or a wrong
singular or plural.

### 1. Onboarding and purchase

You just installed the app. Go through onboarding:
- Allow notifications and allow alarms right away, both on the app's own screens
  and on the system alerts.
- On the first paywall, choose the **Monthly** plan (1 month, "Mesečno" in
  Serbian) and buy it. Confirm the purchase sheet.

### 2. Categories and subcategories

Open Add Medication, type any random medication name, and go to the
medication-type screen.
- Read every category under Common Types (Tablet, Capsule, Oral liquid, Drops,
  Injection) and More Types (Inhaler, Spray, Patch, Ampoule, Infusion, Oral,
  Rectal, Vaginal, Cream / Gel / Ointment, Powder / Granules, Solution /
  Suspension, Skin / Hygiene, Suppository / Insert, Implant / System, Other).
  There must be exactly these 20, each with an icon and a translated title, and
  none of these old names as categories of their own: Syrup, Eye dropper, Nasal
  spray, Ointment, Pestle, Vitamine, Mouthwash, Suppository, Solution,
  Suspension, Other type.
- Open every category that has subcategories and read the whole list, scrolling
  to the end. Every subcategory needs a translated label, no duplicates, and it
  must fit its category (for example, Nasal Spray under Spray, Mouthwash under
  Oral, Eye Drops under Drops, Ointment under Cream / Gel / Ointment). Go back
  without choosing, except where step 3 needs a choice.

### 3. Add five medications, all at 08:00 daily

Any random names are fine for medications 1-4. Before Done, check the dose unit
on the dose step and on the summary for both one unit and more than one.
1. Random name, Tablet › Oral Tablet, 2 tablets (also try 1 and 1.5 before
   setting 2).
2. Another random name, Tablet, 1 tablet.
3. Random name, Oral liquid › Oral Solution, 1 tablespoon, then 2 tablespoons.
4. Random name, Drops › Eye Drops, 1 drop, then 3 drops.
5. Catalog medication: type `Fluorinse` on the name step and pick the
   suggestion. This brand name is the one exception to the rule of entering
   only made-up names: it is needed to check a medication from the app's
   catalog, and no personal data is involved. It must land in Oral › Mouthwash with the **mouthwash** dose unit.
   Set 2.
If the step budget is getting low, skip the subcategory lists you already read
rather than a medication.

### 4. Cross-checks

- Schedule tab, today, 08:00: the group summary must name the forms with the
  right number, for example "Tablets, oral liquid and 2 more" (two tablets give
  the plural "Tablets"; one oral liquid stays singular). Expand the group and
  read each row's dose ("2 tablets", "1 tablet", "2 tablespoons", "3 drops",
  and the mouthwash dose in the app's wording).
- Medications tab: each medication shows its category icon and title, and its
  details show the right category, subcategory, and dose unit.

### Rules

Strictly never tap these, anywhere:
- Open iPhone Settings, Open Settings, Manage Alarms, Manage Notifications,
- Cancel Subscription, Manage Subscriptions, Restore Purchases,
- Rate us, Contact us, Share, Print.
Never leave the app and never press Home. If another app comes to the front
anyway, go straight back with the "◀ MedsTime" link in the top-left corner.
If Privacy Policy or Terms of Service opens, close it with the X button.

Report as a finding every raw key, missing translation, wrong singular or
plural, mixed languages on one screen, missing or placeholder icon, duplicated
or misplaced subcategory, and any old category name that still appears.

## Done when

All 20 categories and their subcategory lists were read, the five medications
were added and checked on the dose step and the summary, and the Schedule group
summary and the Medications tab were checked.

## Persona

Organized, careful user who reads every label before tapping, checks lists to
the end, and notices small wording mistakes. Not tech-savvy.
